## Goal

Design a 3D-printed chassis and suspension for the rover with four-wheel independent steering
(4WIS, one servo per corner) that can **spin in place** and **crab walk**, printed in sections on
a LulzBot Mini.

## Constraints

| Constraint | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Printer bed | 152 × 152 × 158 mm (LulzBot Mini) | stock spec |
| Design envelope | **140 × 140 × 145 mm** per part | chosen safety margin |
| Wheels | **120 mm Ø × 42 mm**, 12 mm hex, hub pocket **81 mm Ø** | measured |
| Hex seat | hex mating face is **33 mm** in from the wheel's inner (motor-side) face | measured |
| Drive motors | JGA25-370 + encoder, 25 mm Ø, one per wheel, steers with the wheel | in hand |
| Motor length | gearbox face → hex mating face **22 mm**; gearbox + motor **51 mm**, + **10 mm** encoder clearance → **83 mm** hex face to encoder back | measured |
| Battery | 4S4P 18650, 144 × 65 × 36 mm (two layers of 8 cells) | in hand |
| Steering servos | **MG996R** (in hand). MG90S also in hand but too weak (see Section 2) | in hand |

From these, the gearbox face sits 11 mm inside the hub pocket and **50 mm** of motor sticks out
past the wheel's inner face.

## Decisions so far

1. **Steering range: ±45° commanded, ±50° mechanical clearance.** True 90° sideways crab was
   rejected: it needs the servo's full throw and a much larger clearance pocket per corner.
2. **Corner architecture: option B (kingpin inboard of the wheel), 2026-09-25.** The kingpin runs
   vertically through the part of the motor that sticks out, 18 mm inboard of the wheel.
   - A **knuckle** clamps the motor and steers. It turns on an 8 mm pin in **nylon bushings**
     (no ball bearings) in the top and bottom plates of a **C-shaped upright**.
   - The **MG996R** sits on top of the upright, output on the kingpin, body running inboard.
   - The upright moves with the suspension but doesn't steer. The double wishbones bolt to its
     back, which sits 41 mm inboard of the kingpin to clear the motor tail's 34 mm sweep.
   - Arms sit at about axle height: lower joint at z ≈ 38 mm, upper at z ≈ 87 mm.
   - The servo top is at about 125 mm, level with the 120 mm tire top.
   - **Option A rejected:** a kingpin through the tire center (zero scrub) with the suspension
     above the wheel. With a 120 mm wheel it stacks the servo and arms to about 195 mm, and the
     pivot towers no longer fit the 145 mm print height.
3. **Layout: 320 × 230** (track tire-center to tire-center × wheelbase).
   - With 39 mm scrub, the contact patch swings around the kingpin as the wheel steers. That
     makes the spin angle larger than `atan(WB/T)`, so the study solves it numerically.
   - The table below shows why 300 × 240 and 300 × 220 were dropped.
4. **Battery lengthwise (144 mm side fore-aft), low, on the centerline**, between the front and
   rear spines and held by two side cradles about 136 mm long. The sides beside it are left open
   for the motor drivers, servo regulator and Blue Pill.
5. **Front and rear modules are narrow spines**, not full 140 mm boxes. Each carries the wishbone
   inner pivots at ±22 mm (lower) and ±28 mm (upper) from the centerline, plus the shock towers.
   The center section holds the battery. Two lengthwise rails (carbon tube or aluminum) join them.
   Front and rear spines are the same part.

| 300 × 220 | 44.7° | 48 / 42 | arms short once the 83 mm motor was measured |
| **320 × 230** | **43.5°** | **58 / 52** | **chosen** |

## Section 2: Corner module (PROPOSED, awaiting sign-off)

```
 chassis spine ─ lower / upper wishbones ─ UPRIGHT C (moves with suspension, doesn't steer)
                                           ├ top plate: MG996R + nylon bushing
                                           ├ bottom plate: nylon bushing
                                           └ KNUCKLE on 8 mm pin, ~40 mm between bushings (steers)
                                               └ clamps the 50 mm motor stub → JGA25 → hex → wheel
```

| Part | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kingpin bushings | 8 mm steel pin in two flanged nylon or PTFE bushings (8×10 mm), about 40 mm apart, with a nylon thrust washer taking the weight | Bushings develop play, so spacing them far apart matters. They are pressed or reamed into printed bores |
| Servo coupling | Metal 25T horn screwed to a clamp hub on top of the pin; servo mounting holes slotted for alignment | The servo spline carries only steering torque, never wheel load or bumps |
| Motor mount | Knuckle clamps the 50 mm motor stub. Optional: a sleeve into the 81 mm hub pocket bolted to the gearbox face | The gearbox face is 11 mm inside the pocket |
| Steering stops | Printed hard stops at ±52° | Protect the servo and wiring from overshoot |
| Wiring | Motor and encoder lead runs as a short service loop near the kingpin axis, then along the lower arm; silicone wire | Near the axis, ±50° only twists the wire instead of pulling it |

- **Servo torque:** the rover is estimated at about 2.8 kg, so about 6.9 N per wheel. Steering
  while stopped at 39 mm scrub takes about 0.8 × 6.9 × 0.039 ≈ **0.23 N·m**.
  - The **MG996R** (about 0.9–1.1 N·m at 6 V) has 3–4× margin.
  - The **MG90S** (about 0.2 N·m stall) would stall. It could be reused for a sensor pan/tilt.
- **Servo power:** 4 × ~2.5 A stall needs a dedicated **5–6 V, ≥8 A regulator**, separate from
  the logic buck converter, so steering spikes don't brown out the Blue Pill.
- **Ground clearance** is modest: the lower bushing plate is about 34 mm up and the belly about
  32 mm. Raising it means moving the whole corner up.
