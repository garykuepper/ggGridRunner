## Goal

Design a 3D-printed chassis and suspension for the rover with four-wheel independent steering
(4WIS, one servo per corner) that can **spin in place** and **crab walk**, printed in sections on
a LulzBot Mini.

## Constraints

| Constraint | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Printer bed | 152 × 152 × 158 mm (LulzBot Mini) | stock spec |
| Design envelope | **140 × 140 × 145 mm** per part | chosen safety margin |
| Wheels | **120 mm Ø × 42 mm**, 12 mm hex, hub pocket **81 mm Ø** | measured |
| Hex seat | hex mating face is **33 mm** in from the wheel's inner (motor-side) face | measured |
| Drive motors | JGA25-370 + encoder, 25 mm Ø, one per wheel, steers with the wheel | in hand |
| Motor length | gearbox face → hex mating face **22 mm**; gearbox + motor **51 mm** + **10 mm** encoder clearance = **61 mm** behind the gearbox face | measured |
| Battery | 4S4P 18650, 144 × 65 × 36 mm (two layers of 8 cells) | in hand |
| Steering servos | **MG996R** (in hand). MG90S also in hand but not used | in hand |
| Joints | **No ball joints** (hard to print); pins in nylon bushings only | user preference |
| Unsprung mass | Keep it small: the servo stays on the chassis | user preference |
| Reference | the user's orange printed prototype corner (photos, 2026-09-25) | in hand |

From these, the gearbox face sits **11 mm inside the hub pocket**, and 50 mm of motor sticks out
past the wheel's inner face.

## Decisions so far

1. **Steering range: ±45° commanded, ±46° mechanical clearance, hard stops at ±47°.**
   - Spin in place needs 41.5° at 280 × 230.
   - The 81 mm hub pocket is what limits the steering range: the arms, and the tie rod where it
     passes the pocket rim, are what run out of room at full lock.
2. **Keep the unsprung parts small** (the user's direction, study 07). Only the knuckle, motor,
   wheel and two small **kingpin blocks** move with the wheel.
   - **Knuckle plate:** bolts to the gearbox face inside the hub pocket.
   - **Kingpin blocks:** two, at z = 86 and 32 mm. Each has a fore-aft pin to its arm's stem and a
     vertical kingpin pin to the knuckle. Two plain pins make a universal joint, with no ball
     joints. Scrub is 10 mm.
3. **Arms: boomerangs, 95 mm, parallel, with a single 35 mm stem at the knuckle**, then two legs
   to the spine pivots at ±35 mm.
   - **Stem:** a single stem is what passes the pocket rim at full lock; spread legs catch it.
     The arm section there is 7 mm.
   - **Chassis rise:** the inner pivots sit **15 mm** above the knuckle hinges, so the chassis
     rides higher (belly about 39 mm) and the wheels hang lower.
   - **Upper arm:** climbs gently from the knuckle to a bend 76 mm out, then runs flat. It has to
     stay low until it is out of the pocket.
   - **Lower arm:** runs flat under the whole motor, then climbs from 66 mm out. The two arms are
     **different parts**: an exact mirror of the upper arm would put the lower arm's climb under
     the motor, which hits at droop.
4. **Steering: MG996R on the spine**, between the upper and lower arm pivots.
   - **Mounting:** the body lies along the arm, and the horn sits on a 12 mm standoff so the tie
     rod passes over the body.
   - **Linkage:** a 12 mm horn and a 12 mm knuckle steering arm, with the tie rod the same length
     as the arms and the same 15 mm rise. That makes a 1:1 parallelogram with near-zero bump
     steer.
   - **Tie rod ends:** pin universals, no balls.
   - **Motor gap:** because the tie rod rises 15 mm, the same as the bump travel, its gap to the
     motor never shrinks.
5. **Shock** from a 10 mm boss on the upper arm, 40 mm in from the knuckle, to a central tower
   (top mount z ≈ 164), 100 mm eye to eye.
6. **Battery lengthwise (144 mm side fore-aft)**, low, on the centerline, held by two side cradles.
7. **Front and rear spines** carry the arm pivots, the servos and the shock tower; the center
   section holds the battery. Two lengthwise rails join them. Front and rear spines are the same
   part.

## Full-bump check (study 08)

A true 3D, surface-to-surface check of every pair that could touch. It covers every steering
angle to ±46° and the whole travel, +15 / −10 mm. **Nothing touches; the smallest gap is 2.2 mm.**

| Pair | Smallest gap |
|---|---|
| Tie rod / upper arm | 2.2 mm (droop, full lock) |
| Wheel (pocket rim) / upper arm | 2.4 mm (droop, full lock) |
| Servo / lower arm | 3.1 mm |
| Wheel / lower arm | 3.3 mm |
| Motor / tie rod | 3.5 mm, constant through the travel |
| Wheel / tie rod | 3.7 mm |
| Servo / tie rod | 4.7 mm |
| Motor / lower arm | 5.5 mm (droop) |
| Everything else | 8 mm or more |

- **Shock length:** 103.6 mm at droop, 94.2 mm at bump.
- **Scrub with travel:** the knuckle moves 1.2 mm outboard at full bump.

**How these values were found:** a parameter search over the upper-block height, upper-arm bend,
tie rod height, steering-arm length, horn standoff, rise and steering limit. At ±50°, or with a
20 mm rise, the upper arm hits the pocket rim at full droop. The chosen set is the one that
clears everything by at least 2 mm.

## Section 2: Corner module (PROPOSED, awaiting sign-off)

```
 spine ─ upper boomerang (2 legs → stem) ─ fore-aft pin ─ UPPER BLOCK ─ kingpin pin ─┐
       ├ MG996R (standoff horn) ─ tie rod (pin U-joints, 1:1) ─ steering arm ────────┤ KNUCKLE PLATE
       └ lower boomerang (2 legs → stem) ─ fore-aft pin ─ LOWER BLOCK ─ kingpin pin ─┘  └ JGA25 → hex → wheel
         shock: upper arm boss → central tower
```

| Part | Proposal | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Kingpin blocks | 10 mm printed blocks: a fore-aft pin to the arm stem, a vertical pin into a knuckle boss, flanged nylon bushings | Pin universal; no ball joints |
| Arm hinges at the spine | Fore-aft pins in nylon bushings | Hinges only |
| Arms | Two boomerang parts (upper, lower), 7 mm section at the stem | The stem must pass the pocket rim |
| Steering | MG996R on the spine, metal 25T horn on a 12 mm standoff, tie rod with pin-universal ends | 1:1 parallelogram, near-zero bump steer |
| Steering stops | Printed hard stops at ±47° | The 3D check is clean to ±46° |
| Shock | Standard eyes on pins; lower eye on a 10 mm boss on the upper arm | Keeps the shock body off the arm |
| Motor wiring | Service loop near the kingpin axis, then along the lower arm | Near the axis, steering only twists the wire instead of pulling it |

- **Servo torque:** steering while stopped at 10 mm scrub takes about 0.06 N·m, plus contact-patch
  twist. The MG996R's ~1 N·m has a large margin.
- **Servo power:** a dedicated **5–6 V, ≥8 A regulator**, separate from the logic buck converter.

## Open questions

- Section 2 sign-off: pin and bushing sizes, block and stem sections, and the ±47° stops.
- Wheel travel split: +15 / −10 mm assumed. Confirm against the shocks.
- Rise: 15 mm. 20 mm would lift the chassis further, but then the upper arm hits the pocket rim
  at droop.
- Printer Z height: confirm against the actual machine (158 mm stock).
